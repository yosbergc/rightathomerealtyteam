## How to increase your home's value before you list

The temptation before selling is to renovate. New kitchen, new bathrooms, new everything. In most cases, that's the wrong instinct. Major renovations rarely return their full cost at resale. What does move the needle is a targeted set of improvements that make the home show well and signal to buyers that it's been cared for.

## Start with cleanliness and declutter

No improvement returns more per dollar spent than a deep clean and a serious declutter. Buyers need to be able to imagine their life in the space, which is difficult when the current owner's life is visible everywhere. Remove personal items, reduce furniture to what's necessary, and clear countertops. It costs almost nothing and has an outsized effect on how buyers perceive the home.

## Address deferred maintenance

Fix the things you've been meaning to fix. The leaky faucet. The door that doesn't close properly. The cracked tile. The missing lightbulb. These small items, when accumulated, send a signal to buyers that the home hasn't been maintained, and they start to wonder what else has been neglected. A home that's in good repair feels safe. One with visible deferred maintenance does not.

## Focus on curb appeal

First impressions are formed before buyers walk through the door. Mow the lawn, trim the hedges, power wash the driveway, repaint the front door if it needs it. Fresh mulch in garden beds costs very little and makes an immediate visual difference. Buyers who like what they see from the street arrive inside already positively disposed.

## Paint strategically

Fresh paint in neutral tones is one of the highest-return pre-listing investments. It makes spaces feel clean, larger, and move-in ready. Focus on the main living areas and the primary bedroom. Avoid bold or highly personal color choices, because buyers are trying to see themselves in the home, not you.

## Know what not to do

Skip the full kitchen renovation. Skip the bathroom gut. Skip the addition. Unless the home is severely dated relative to comparable properties, these projects rarely return their investment, and they take time you may not have.
