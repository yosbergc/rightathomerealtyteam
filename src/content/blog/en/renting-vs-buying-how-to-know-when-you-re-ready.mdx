## Renting vs buying: how to know when you're ready

The question of whether to rent or buy comes up constantly, and it rarely has a clean answer. The honest truth is that buying isn't always the right move, and renting isn't always throwing money away. Here's how to think through it clearly.

## The financial case for buying

When you own a home, your monthly payments build equity rather than going entirely to a landlord. Over time, and assuming the market holds reasonably steady, homeownership tends to build wealth in a way that renting doesn't. You also gain the ability to lock in a fixed mortgage payment, which provides protection against rising rents that renters don't have.

The financial case for buying is strongest when you plan to stay in the home for at least five years. Buying and selling in a short window rarely pencils out once you factor in closing costs, agent commissions, and transaction friction.

## The financial case for renting

Renting preserves flexibility and keeps your capital liquid. If your job situation is uncertain, if you're new to an area and still learning where you want to be, or if the local market is significantly overvalued relative to rents, renting can be the smarter short-term choice.

The cost of a mortgage, including taxes, insurance, and maintenance, often exceeds the cost of renting a comparable property. The difference in monthly outlay matters if you're investing the surplus thoughtfully.

## The non-financial factors

Stability, school districts, the ability to renovate and personalize, and the sense of permanence that comes with ownership all have real value that doesn't show up in a spreadsheet. For many people, these factors weigh as heavily as the numbers.

## A simple test

Ask yourself three questions: Do I plan to stay here for at least five years? Is my income stable enough to carry a mortgage comfortably, not just technically? Am I buying because it makes sense for my life, or because I feel like I'm supposed to?

If the answers are yes, yes, and the first one, you're probably ready.
