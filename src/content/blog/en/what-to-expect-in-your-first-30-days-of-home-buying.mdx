## What to expect in your first 30 days of home buying

Buying a home for the first time can feel like stepping into a process that everyone else already understands. They don't. Most first-time buyers figure it out as they go — which is fine, but expensive when the learning curve hits at the wrong moment. Here's what the first 30 days actually look like.

## Days 1–7: Get your finances in order

Before you look at a single listing, talk to a lender. Getting pre-approved tells you exactly what you can spend — and it tells sellers you're a serious buyer, not a browser. Bring your last two years of tax returns, recent pay stubs, and bank statements. The pre-approval process usually takes 3 to 5 business days.

Don't make any large purchases or open new credit lines during this period. Lenders re-check your credit before closing, and changes to your financial profile can delay or derail the process.

## Days 8–14: Define what you actually need

Write two lists. The first is non-negotiable, the things a home must have. The second is everything else. Buyers who skip this step waste weeks viewing homes that were never right for them.

Think about commute time, school district, parking, outdoor space, and storage. Think about what your life looks like in five years, not just today. Your agent will use this list to filter listings before you ever see them.

## Days 15–21: Start viewing homes

Your agent will schedule viewings based on your criteria. Plan to see 6 to 10 homes in your first round. Take notes and photos at each one, they blur together faster than you'd expect.

Pay attention to things that can't be changed: lot size, street noise, natural light, ceiling height, neighbor proximity. Everything else, paint, fixtures, landscaping, is cosmetic and replaceable.

## Days 22–30: Evaluate and prepare to move

By the end of the first month, most buyers have a clear front-runner. If you find a home you want to offer on, your agent will prepare a comparative market analysis to help you understand what it's actually worth before you name a number.

Don't rush the offer, but don't overthink it either. In a competitive market, well-priced homes move fast. Trust your preparation, you've done the work.
