5 things that actually affect your home's sale price Every seller wants to maximize what their home sells for. Most focus on staging and paint colors. Those things matter, but they're not what drives price. Here are the five factors that actually determine what a buyer is willing to pay.

## Listing price

Counterintuitively, overpricing your home often results in a lower final sale price. Homes that sit on the market attract less interest and invite low offers. Buyers assume something is wrong. A well-researched listing price, based on recent comparable sales, not wishful thinking, generates more activity, more competition, and stronger offers.

## Days on market

The longer a home sits, the more negotiating power shifts to the buyer. Homes that sell in the first two weeks typically sell closest to asking price. After 30 days, buyers start asking why and using that as leverage. This is why pricing correctly from day one matters more than any renovation you could do.

## Condition of the home at first showing

Buyers decide within minutes. The entry, the kitchen, and the primary bedroom carry the most weight. Deep cleaning, decluttering, and minor repairs (a leaky faucet, a sticking door, cracked caulking) signal to buyers that the home has been cared for. Deferred maintenance signals the opposite.

## Photography and listing presentation

Over 90% of buyers search online before stepping inside a home. Low-quality photos lose buyers before they ever schedule a viewing. Professional photography, accurate floor plans, and a well-written listing description are not optional because they are the first showing.

## Your agent's negotiation approach

The gap between your asking price and final sale price is often determined by how your agent handles the offer process. Multiple offer situations, escalation clauses, contingency terms, and closing timelines all affect the net number you walk away with. An experienced agent earns their commission, and then some, in this stage alone.
