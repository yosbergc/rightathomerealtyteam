## How to make a strong offer without overpaying

In a market where good homes attract multiple offers, buyers face a dilemma: go high enough to win, but not so high that you overpay. There's no formula that works every time, but there are principles that consistently give buyers an edge.

## Start with the data, not the emotion

Before you write a number, look at what comparable homes have sold for in the past 90 days. Your agent should pull this analysis for you. The asking price is an opinion. Recent closed sales are facts. Understanding the difference between the two is the foundation of every smart offer.

## Get pre-approved, not just pre-qualified

Pre-approval means a lender has reviewed your financials and committed to lending you a specific amount. Pre-qualification is a rough estimate based on self-reported information. Sellers and their agents know the difference. A pre-approval letter makes your offer significantly more credible, especially when competing against other buyers.

## Think beyond the price

The offer price is one variable. Sellers also weigh closing timeline, contingencies, and deposit size. A buyer offering slightly below asking with a flexible closing date and a clean contract can beat a higher offer that's loaded with conditions. Ask your agent what matters to the seller, because sometimes a short close or a rent-back arrangement is worth more to them than an extra $10,000.

## Don't waive inspections blindly

In competitive markets, some buyers waive inspection contingencies to strengthen their offer. This carries real risk. A better approach is to do a pre-offer walkthrough with a contractor, so you understand the home's condition before you write, and can make an informed decision about contingency terms rather than a pressured one.

## Know your walkaway number before you start

Decide your maximum number before you get emotionally attached to a home. Write it down. Buying a home you love is a good outcome. Buying a home you love and then resenting the price for the next five years is not. Discipline before the offer is far easier than regret after.
